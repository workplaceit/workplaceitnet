GCC High vs GCC vs Commercial Microsoft 365: Which Do You Need for CMMC?

November 6, 2026 • 8 min read • By Workplace IT

Choosing the wrong Microsoft 365 environment is one of the most expensive CMMC mistakes a defense contractor can make. The right answer depends on one thing first: what kind of data you handle.

For most small and mid-sized defense contractors, Microsoft 365 is where email, files, and collaboration live, which means it's also where Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) ends up. Microsoft offers three main clouds for this market: Commercial, GCC (Government Community Cloud), and GCC High. They look similar to users, but they are built for very different data.

Start With Your Data, Not the License

Before comparing products, answer three questions:

  1. Do you only handle Federal Contract Information (FCI)? That typically points to CMMC Level 1.
  2. Do you handle CUI? Then you're looking at CMMC Level 2, and any cloud service that stores or processes CUI must meet FedRAMP Moderate or equivalent.
  3. Is any of it export-controlled, such as ITAR or EAR data? That narrows the options further.

Microsoft's own guidance recommends GCC High for organizations that are planning for or required to meet CMMC Level 2 and above, and it is the standard choice when ITAR or EAR data is in play. GCC can work in some CUI scenarios, but it is not suitable for export-controlled data, so the decision depends on exactly what you handle and what your contracts require. Microsoft's guidance in this area changes, so confirm the current documentation for your situation before you buy.

Commercial Microsoft 365

Commercial Microsoft 365 is fine for businesses that only handle FCI, and with proper configuration it can support CMMC Level 1. It is generally not where you want CUI to live. If you handle CUI today on commercial Microsoft 365, that's a gap to address before an assessment.

GCC

GCC keeps data in the U.S. and is designed for government and contractor workloads. For some organizations with CUI and no export-controlled data, it may be an option. The tradeoffs and requirements need to be reviewed against your specific contracts, which is exactly the kind of decision that's worth getting right before you migrate.

GCC High

GCC High is a separate, U.S. sovereign cloud supported by screened U.S. persons, built for defense contractors handling CUI and export-controlled data. It costs more, it requires eligibility validation, and some third-party applications don't support it. For many contractors handling CUI, it's still the clearest path to Level 2.

The Enclave Option

Not everyone in your company needs to be in GCC High. If CUI only touches part of the business, such as an engineering or contracts team, an enclave can limit the high-security environment to the people and systems that actually handle CUI. That can reduce cost and disruption. It also adds complexity, because your scoping and documentation have to clearly define the boundary.

What a GCC High Migration Really Involves

Moving to GCC High is not a license switch. It's a new tenant in a separate cloud, and your data, identities, and devices have to be moved into it. A typical project includes:

  • Eligibility and licensing: validation and purchase through an authorized partner
  • Tenant build: identity, MFA, Conditional Access, and security baselines configured from day one
  • Device management: enrolling and securing endpoints with Intune
  • Data migration: email, OneDrive, SharePoint, and Teams content moved from the old tenant
  • Application review: confirming each third-party app works in GCC High, or finding alternatives
  • Documentation: updating your SSP and policies to reflect the new environment

Plan the cutover: because it's a new tenant, users get new sign-ins and devices need to be re-enrolled. A clear cutover plan and user communication keep the business running during the move.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need GCC High for CMMC Level 2?

Not by name. CMMC requires that cloud services storing or processing CUI meet FedRAMP Moderate or equivalent. Microsoft recommends GCC High for organizations planning for Level 2 and above, and it is the standard choice when ITAR or EAR data is involved.

Can I use commercial Microsoft 365 for CMMC?

For FCI and CMMC Level 1, commercial Microsoft 365 can work with proper configuration. It is generally not recommended for storing or processing CUI.

Can I convert my existing Microsoft 365 tenant to GCC High?

No. GCC High is a separate cloud, so it requires a new tenant and a migration of users, data, and devices.

Does everyone in my company need GCC High?

Not necessarily. If only part of the business handles CUI, an enclave can limit GCC High to those users and systems, as long as the boundary is clearly scoped and documented.
